# Challenge to feed 10 billion by 2050

- [cite:searchingerCreatingSustainableFood2019](cite:searchingerCreatingSustainableFood2019) suggests that this goal is possible but there are many specific challenge areas, such as reducing food waste, and increasing production without increasing land use.
- Aside from reforesting unproductive agricultural areas, we also need to protect and restore peatlands.
- We also need to reduce the damaging affect from pesticide and fertiliser run-off into natural eco-systems.

# Trends for AI in agriculture

- Initial work in solving specific problems - such as killing weeds with lasers.
- Holistic approaches to whole community solutions. Need to make it work for the community - not focus on techno-solutions.
- Google maps parallel - need solution that is equivalent - makes every farmer small percentage more efficient.
- As opposed to more techno solutions - lasering weeds - which may only be viable in limited situations.

# Points

- Solutions for robotic pickers and crop yield assessment
- Climate change and agriculture production pressure are interlinked. Poorer countries clear land leading to widespread deforestation and then use that land for beef production, which contributes to methane production. \[cite:@FutureFarmingArtificial2020\]
- AI can help create food production systems that require a smaller space and use less resources.
- AI has a cost—training a model may cause significant carbon emissions due to the data centre electricity for computation and cooling.
- AI could be divisive—AI have and have nots. AI may encourage more centralised, conglomerate farming approaches and harm small independent farmers.
- Farm workers may lose their jobs if their labour is replaced with automation.

## Practical applications

- Crop and soil monitoring: use drones to capture aerial image data and then train computer vision models to detect crop health and soil malnutrition issues. \[cite:@AIAgriculturePractical\]
- Detect ripeness—computer vision models can help also with detecting when fruit is ripe.
- Pest infestation and disease (such as mould) can be detected using computer vision approaches.
- Livestock monitoring, based on computer vision – cattle, chickens being monitored.

## Variations between countries

- Variable rate application of nutrients and crop protection products (VRT) \[cite:@shahebPrecisionAgricultureSustainable2022\].
- "Right" management practices—right amount in the right place at the right time from the right source in the right manner.
