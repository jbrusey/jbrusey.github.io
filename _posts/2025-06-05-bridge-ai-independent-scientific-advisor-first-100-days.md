# First 100 days

The "first 100 days" is usually a phrase reserved for political leaders—presidents, or prime ministers (if they last). It marks the end of the honeymoon and an opportunity to ask: Did anything really happen, or was it all just talk? Is the promise starting to turn into reality?

This blog will briefly describe my reflections on my first 100 days as an Independent Scientific Adviser (ISA). This period has been exciting and challenging allowing me to connect with real-world problems. To keep things brief, I'm going to skip over the technical details.

# What was promised?

From the side of the Alan Turing Institute, the promise was clear: deliver enough industry clients to keep us Independent Scientific Advisers (ISAs) busy. They certainly delivered! I have had more engagement with industry in these few months than at any other point in my academic career.

From the ISA side, the promise was more ambitious: to bring AI to every SME that asked for it. To solve problems, to unstick processes, and to inject cutting edge methods into the heart of industry. The reality, though, has been more prosaic. Most businesses aren't ready for state-of-the-art AI. The real needs are more basic—such as, how can we address fears about privacy and security of client data?

But this doesn't mean that the ISA can't make an impact. AI, and Generative AI in particular, holds real promise to improve processes and the working lives of many knowledge workers. For many, though, that promise is still just that—a promise. Figuring out how to make the best use of AI is a journey that many have only just begun.

# It's not consultancy

It’s easy to slip into the role of a consultant: What’s the client’s problem? How can my skills fix it? Don’t get up—let me pass you the remote.

It took time to realise that being an ISA is more like being a coach. Coaches are demanding—because real transformation needs more than providing a specification. It needs commitment, effort, and learning. New skills are hard to master, and lasting change doesn’t come from a report, no matter how well written.

# Is it technical?

Does the coach need a PhD? What is the value of the technical depth? Sometimes, it's important to know when *not* to apply a highly technical approach. However, coming from a position of deep understanding is also helpful. AI is having its "medical degree" moment—when it transitions from something that any snake oil salesman can do to something that is understood to require proper training and experience.

# Was the first 100 days a success?

So—was my first 100 days as a BridgeAI ISA a success? The real impact will take much longer to judge. In the meantime, I’ve been using a simpler, more immediate heuristic: *Am I learning? Am I growing in my abilities?* On that basis, it’s been a resounding success.
